## What it is

VSAT-XL (Extra Large Vertical Solar Array Technology) is a giant, sun-tracking solar tower for the Moon's south pole, designed by [Astrobotic](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-awarded-lunar-power-study-with-vsat-xl/) of Pittsburgh. It is meant to produce 50 kW, five times its 10 kW sibling, the VSAT. In the Phase I study it was 34 m tall and 12.5 m wide, which Astrobotic called the largest planned lunar power infrastructure technology to date. Like the VSAT, it is described as deployable, relocatable, self-leveling and sun-tracking. Its solar blankets are [Roll-Out Solar Arrays (ROSA)](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-awarded-lunar-power-study-with-vsat-xl/) from Redwire Space.

NASA funded it through its Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) program. The Phase I purchase order was worth [$148,394](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_80NSSC24PB267_8000_-NONE-_-NONE-) and ran about six months from August 2024 to February 2025, according to the [award record](https://www.highergov.com/contract/80NSSC24PB267/). Phase I set out to find a stable design and fit it on a current-generation launch vehicle, [Astrobotic says](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-developing-xl-solar-array-tech-for-lunar-power-infrastructure/). The Phase II contract is worth [$849,694](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_80NSSC25C0032_8000_-NONE-_-NONE-) and runs from 5 June 2025 to 5 June 2027. Under it, Astrobotic will build a prototype to test the solar array deployment system. (Astrobotic's Phase II release is dated 15 July 2025, but its body text says "July 15, 2024", which looks like a typo.)

As of November 2026, no test results from the Phase II prototype have been published. The tower is a ground-development project. No flight is scheduled.

## Why Open Moon uses it

Open Moon's power milestone (M04) plans two 50 kW VSAT-XL towers alongside two 10 kW VSATs and a fission reactor. The towers supply the bulk of the solar power for the oxygen pilot plant and the habitat. Astrobotic itself names the target loads: [pressurized rovers, habitats and ISRU pilot plants](https://www.highergov.com/contract/80NSSC24PB267/).

Height is the point. At the poles the Sun stays close to the horizon, and terrain can block light from low arrays. Masts lift the arrays into steadier light, [NASA explains](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/three-companies-to-help-nasa-advance-solar-array-technology-for-moon/). VSAT-XL is part of Astrobotic's [LunaGrid](https://www.astrobotic.com/lunar-delivery/lunar-surface-power/) power service. That service also uses cable-laying rovers and wireless chargers to carry power to users. Astrobotic and Honda are also studying how to pair [VSAT and VSAT-XL with Honda's regenerative fuel cell](https://www.astrobotic.com/honda-and-astrobotic-establish-joint-development-agreement-to-explore-scalable-lunar-power-solutions/) to get through dark periods.

Without VSAT-XL, Open Moon would need about five times as many 10 kW towers to reach the same solar capacity. The alternative is to rely more on a fission reactor that does not exist yet. Astrobotic says no existing lunar power solution meets needs [at the 50 kW scale and above](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-awarded-lunar-power-study-with-vsat-xl/).

## How it works

The [Phase I abstract](https://www.highergov.com/contract/80NSSC24PB267/) describes several ROSA wings linked into one "Mega-ROSA". This large array sits on a gimbal that levels it and points it at the Sun. ROSA is a flexible blanket of solar cells held between two rolled composite booms. The booms straighten as they unroll, [like a tape measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roll_Out_Solar_Array), so the wing needs no motor to deploy in orbit. For the 10 kW VSAT, Redwire added [dual motors](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwires-roll-out-solar-arrays-to-enable-lunar-power-infrastructure-for-astrobotic-vsat-program/) so the array can deploy and retract vertically many times on the Moon. Astrobotic has not said whether VSAT-XL uses the same motors.

In the [Phase II design](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-developing-xl-solar-array-tech-for-lunar-power-infrastructure/), retractable solar blankets 20 m long are raised more than 10 m above the ground, and the tower mounts on top of a Griffin lander. A mobile version on a self-driving base could [drive to new sites](https://www.highergov.com/contract/80NSSC24PB267/) without needing a separate rover. Linking several towers could scale output well beyond 50 kW.

## Limitations and open issues

- **Early maturity.** NASA has funded only a study and a deployment prototype, about $1M in total ([Phase I](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_80NSSC24PB267_8000_-NONE-_-NONE-), [Phase II](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_80NSSC25C0032_8000_-NONE-_-NONE-)). No test results have been published, and no flight is funded.
- **No published mass.** Astrobotic has not published the tower's mass or stowed size. Open Moon's delivery cost for two towers is therefore an estimate.
- **The smaller version is not flown either.** The 10 kW VSAT prototypes from Astrobotic and Honeybee went through [thermal-vacuum tests at NASA Johnson in 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/from-houston-to-the-moon-johnsons-thermal-vacuum-chamber-tests-lunar-solar-technology/), but none has been to the Moon. NASA's first funded vertical-array pathfinder is now a [component purchase for miniCAST](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-moon-base-transportation-technology-science-projects/), with ROSA arrays from [Redwire](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-selected-for-40-million-contract-to-demonstrate-power-generation-for-nasa-moon-base-program-leveraging-its-innovative-roll-out-solar-array-technology/), not from Astrobotic.
- **Stability.** A 30 m-plus structure must stay stable on sloped ground and survive repeated deployment and retraction. These are the [design challenges NASA named](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/three-companies-to-help-nasa-advance-solar-array-technology-for-moon/) for VSAT. Astrobotic says finding a [stable and practical design](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-developing-xl-solar-array-tech-for-lunar-power-infrastructure/) was the main Phase I task.
- **Dust.** NASA has warned that lunar dust, including dust thrown up by landers and other activity, can [reduce the output of vertical solar arrays](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240003496) at the south pole.
- **Darkness.** Solar towers produce nothing in shadow. Open Moon still needs storage and a reactor. NASA notes that nights near the poles [can last more than 14 Earth days](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/glenn/nasa-seeks-industry-feedback-on-fission-surface-power/).
- **Depends on Griffin.** The Phase II design [mounts on Astrobotic's Griffin lander](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-developing-xl-solar-array-tech-for-lunar-power-infrastructure/), so any lander delay also delays the tower.
