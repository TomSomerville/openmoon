## What it is

Blue Alchemist is Blue Origin's system for turning lunar soil (regolith) into oxygen, metals and solar cells. Blue Origin made it public in February 2023. The company said it had been making solar cells and power-transmission wire from regolith simulant [since 2021](https://www.solardaily.com/reports/Blue_Origin_unveils_Blue_Alchemist_a_technology_that_turns_Moon_dust_into_solar_cells_999.html). The simulant was made to match real lunar soil in chemistry and minerals. Blue Origin calls it an end-to-end, scalable, autonomous and commercial way to make [solar cells from regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin).

In July 2023 NASA picked the project in its sixth Tipping Point round. Blue Origin got about [$34.7 million](https://www.nasa.gov/general/2023-nasa-tipping-point-selections/), the largest of the 11 awards, which together came to about $150 million. Tipping Point deals are milestone-based Space Act Agreements of up to four years. The company must pay at least 10–25% of the [project cost itself](https://www.nasa.gov/press-release/nasa-partners-with-american-companies-on-key-moon-exploration-tech). NASA says the system will extract oxygen, iron, silicon and aluminium from simulant and use them to make solar cells and wire.

In September 2025 Blue Alchemist passed its [Critical Design Review](https://www.blueorigin.com/news/blue-alchemist-hits-major-milestone-toward-permanent-sustainable-lunar-infrastructure). The next step is an autonomous demonstration in a simulated lunar environment, planned for [2026](https://spectrum.ieee.org/blue-origin-molten-regolith-electrolysis). The work is done at Blue Origin's Space Resources Center of Excellence, which the company says has more than 65 staff. Nothing has flown yet. Open Moon rates it TRL 5. That is our own estimate, not a published figure.

## Why Open Moon uses it

Blue Alchemist does two jobs in the plan:

- **Oxygen from rock (M06).** Lunar soil is about 40% oxygen by mass, and this works anywhere on the Moon, with no ice needed. It is one of the two oxygen-from-rock processes in the ISRU pilot. Blue Origin's own pitch: "each kilogram of oxygen we make on the lunar surface is one less that we have to [launch from Earth](https://spectrum.ieee.org/blue-origin-molten-regolith-electrolysis)."
- **Silicon, metals and solar cells (M12).** Making solar cells, cover glass and aluminium wire on the Moon is the main way Open Moon hopes to grow power beyond what can be shipped. Today the only option is to land every array, such as Astrobotic's planned [50 kW VSAT-XL](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-developing-xl-solar-array-tech-for-lunar-power-infrastructure/).

Without it, oxygen from rock would rest on other processes (see alternatives). Power growth would depend on shipping arrays and reactors from Earth, the most expensive part of the power milestone (M04).

## How it works

The core is molten regolith electrolysis (MRE). Regolith is heated until it melts at [above 1,600 °C](https://www.solardaily.com/reports/Blue_Origin_unveils_Blue_Alchemist_a_technology_that_turns_Moon_dust_into_solar_cells_999.html). The melt itself acts as the electrolyte. An electric current splits the oxides. Oxygen gas forms at the anode, and silicon, iron and aluminium collect as liquid metal at the cathode. Each oxygen molecule needs four electrons, so the current going in sets the [maximum oxygen rate](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260006167/downloads/MRE%20GCD%20ICES26_v3.pdf). Blue Origin says the process needs no water, no toxic chemicals and produces [no carbon emissions](https://www.engadget.com/blue-origina-solar-cells-moon-soil-173908801.html).

The silicon is then refined to make solar cells. Cover glass is made from the process by-products. Without that glass, cells would only last days. With it, Blue Origin expects them to work on the surface for [a decade or more](https://www.engadget.com/blue-origina-solar-cells-moon-soil-173908801.html). Aluminium becomes wire.

For scale, a separate NASA-tested MRE reactor (Lunar Resources LR-1) melted 25 kg of simulant at about 1,700 °C in vacuum. It made about 0.07 kg of oxygen per hour, at about 0.3 mol of O₂ per kWh ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/kennedy/nasa-kennedy-breathes-life-into-moon-soil-testing), [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260006167/downloads/MRE%20GCD%20ICES26_v3.pdf)). Blue Alchemist's own figures may differ.

## Limitations and open issues

- **Almost no public performance data.** Blue Origin has not published oxygen output, energy per kilogram, reactor mass or solar cell efficiency. Most numbers above are company claims repeated by the press.
- **Simulant only.** All results so far use [Earth-made simulant](https://www.solardaily.com/reports/Blue_Origin_unveils_Blue_Alchemist_a_technology_that_turns_Moon_dust_into_solar_cells_999.html), not real lunar regolith, and none in lunar gravity.
- **Autonomous demo still ahead.** The CDR is a design milestone. The [2026 demonstration](https://www.blueorigin.com/news/blue-alchemist-hits-major-milestone-toward-permanent-sustainable-lunar-infrastructure) in a simulated lunar environment has not been reported as done. No flight mission has been announced.
- **Hard hardware.** Electrodes and containers must survive molten rock at more than 1,600 °C. NASA's MRE testing needed [special heating and temperature control](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260006167/downloads/MRE%20GCD%20ICES26_v3.pdf) to reach these temperatures.
- **High power need.** Oxygen output is limited by current. At the efficiency NASA measured for its MRE test, a useful plant needs tens of kilowatts or more. That makes it dependent on the M04 power grid.
- **Cell factory is a big step.** Turning silicon into finished, covered and wired solar cells takes many more steps than making oxygen. Doing all of them autonomously on the Moon is unproven.
- **Single vendor.** It is a proprietary Blue Origin system. Open Moon cannot rely on its schedule, so M06 also carries a second oxygen process.
