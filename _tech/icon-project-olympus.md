## What it is

Project Olympus is ICON's effort to build a construction system for the Moon that uses local soil (regolith) as its main building material. ICON is an Austin, Texas company known for 3D-printed houses. It [launched Olympus in October 2020](https://www.iconbuild.com/newsroom/icon-receives-funding-from-nasa-and-launches-project-olympus-to-reach-for-the-stars-with-off-world-construction-system-for-the-moon) with a government SBIR contract that included NASA funding, working with NASA Marshall Space Flight Center. Architecture firms BIG-Bjarke Ingels Group and SEArch+ joined as design partners.

In November 2022 NASA gave ICON a [$57.2 million SBIR Phase III contract](https://www.iconbuild.com/newsroom/icon-to-develop-lunar-surface-construction-system-with-57-2-million-nasa-award) to keep developing Olympus. ICON says the work will inform construction of landing pads, blast shields and roads, and that the contract's final deliverable is meant to be the first construction on another world. ICON works inside NASA's Moon to Mars Planetary Autonomous Construction Technology (MMPACT) project, [led from Marshall](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220012533/downloads/ISRU%20Tech%20Review%202022%20Edmunson%20v2.pdf).

In February 2025, ICON's Olympus team flew its first space payload, [Duneflow](https://www.iconbuild.com/newsroom/icons-project-duneflow-completes-successful-space-flight-with-blue-origin), on Blue Origin's New Shepard. The capsule [simulated lunar gravity for about two minutes](https://www.nasa.gov/stmd-flight-opportunities/flight-summaries/lunar-gravity-simulation-via-suborbital-rocket/). No Olympus hardware has yet worked on the Moon.

## Why Open Moon uses it

Open Moon needs hard surfaces early. Landing pads, blast shields and roads are part of site preparation in M02, and regolith construction of berms, pads and shells continues in M12 manufacturing. Olympus is the most advanced US-funded system aiming to make these from local material rather than shipped concrete or metal. NASA notes the melted regolith is meant to [withstand lunar dust, radiation and temperature extremes](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/nasa-enables-construction-technology-for-moon-and-mars-exploration/).

Without a system like this, landers would keep blasting dust and rock across the site, and every pad would need imported material. The plan would fall back on pavers, blankets or binders shipped from Earth (see alternatives).

## How it works

ICON's process is called Laser Vitreous Material Transformation (VMX). [High-powered lasers melt regolith](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/nasa-enables-construction-technology-for-moon-and-mars-exploration/), which then cools into a strong, ceramic-like, glassy solid. A robot lays it down layer by layer. ICON has also tested [extruding molten regolith](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220012533/downloads/ISRU%20Tech%20Review%202022%20Edmunson%20v2.pdf).

In a 2022-2023 NASA [landing pad down-select](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230008890/downloads/MMPACT%20Jamboree%20Poster%202023%20-%20final.pdf), multi-layer samples were made at 10^-3 torr vacuum and tested for compression at -192 °C and 57 °C, adhesion, thermal cycling and plasma-torch ablation. Only VMX and a cement mortar finished the full test matrix, and NASA judged VMX the best candidate.

## Limitations and open issues

- **No lunar test yet.** MMPACT's first demonstration mission (DM-1) was meant to build small horizontal and vertical "proof of concept" pieces from a lander. A 2021 plan dated it [2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022078/downloads/MMPACT_ASCEND.pdf); a [2024 NASA roadmap](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240004549/downloads/20240422%20JAXA%20Presentation.pdf) starts at 2027. No flight date is announced in the sources here.
- **Key numbers unpublished.** Laser power, energy per square metre, print speed and system mass are not public, so power and delivery needs cannot be sized from data.
- **Simulant gap.** Ground tests use simulants. NASA's own requirements include [validating that Earth testing is a good enough analog](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220012533/downloads/ISRU%20Tech%20Review%202022%20Edmunson%20v2.pdf) for lunar operations.
- **Material refinement.** NASA listed [refinement of VMX material properties](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230008890/downloads/MMPACT%20Jamboree%20Poster%202023%20-%20final.pdf) and pad design optimization as upcoming work.
- **Autonomy and dust.** MMPACT requires [autonomous and remote operation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220012533/downloads/ISRU%20Tech%20Review%202022%20Edmunson%20v2.pdf) on the surface; this has not been shown on the Moon.
- **Single-supplier risk.** The plan leans on one company's proprietary process. Pavers and other [in-situ methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_situ_resource_utilization) are backups.
