## What it is

NUCLEUS (Nutritional Closed-Loop Eco-Unit System) is a modular food production system built by Interstellar Lab, a small company based in Merritt Island, Florida, and led by Barbara Belvisi. It grows fresh microgreens, vegetables, mushrooms and insects to give a space crew fresh food and micronutrients ([NASA, Phase 2](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-winners-announces-final-phase-of-space-food-challenge/)). The company was founded in 2018 and first became known for its inflatable BioPod growing pods and the EBIOS station concept ([STIR](https://www.stirworld.com/see-features-interstellar-labs-biopod-prepares-for-plant-cultivation-on-the-moon)).

NUCLEUS was developed for NASA's Deep Space Food Challenge, a Centennial Challenge run with the Canadian Space Agency from 2021 to 2024. Interstellar Lab was a Phase 1 winner in 2021 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-winners-of-deep-space-food-challenge/)), a Phase 2 winner in May 2023 with $150,000 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-winners-announces-final-phase-of-space-food-challenge/)), and won the **$750,000 grand prize** in August 2024. More than 300 teams from 32 countries took part ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-1-25-million-to-three-teams-at-deep-space-food-finale/)).

After the challenge, Interstellar Lab partnered with Vast. A NUCLEUS spinoff, Eden 1.0, is a plant research unit meant to fly on the Haven-1 commercial station ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/nasa-challenge-winners-cook/)). Haven-1 is now targeted for 2027 ([Vast](https://www.vastspace.com/haven-1); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haven-1)). NUCLEUS itself has not flown.

## Why Open Moon uses it

Open Moon's M10 Growing food milestone needs fresh food grown at the base. NUCLEUS is a candidate for the compact, early part of that job: a set of ready-made modules that give the crew greens, mushrooms and insect protein while the larger buried greenhouses for staple crops are built up.

The challenge it won was set up for exactly this kind of mission: feeding **a crew of four on a three-year round trip with no resupply** ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/marshall/nasa-begins-final-phase-of-3-million-deep-space-food-challenge/)). Without a system like it, the first crews would depend fully on stored food from Earth, which loses nutrients over time and gives little variety.

## How it works

NUCLEUS combines several autonomous phytotrons (sealed plant growth chambers) with environment-controlled greenhouses into one self-sustaining food system ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-1-25-million-to-three-teams-at-deep-space-food-finale/)). Each module controls its own climate and lighting, so different crops and organisms can each get the conditions they need. Waste and inputs move between modules in a closed loop.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Modules | 9 interconnected: 6 for food, 3 for system support ([Payload](https://payloadspace.com/interstellar-lab-wins-deep-space-food-challenge/)) |
| Outputs | Microgreens, vegetables, mushrooms, insects ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-winners-announces-final-phase-of-space-food-challenge/)) |
| Automation | Designed to be 90% automated; crew inspect and maintain ([Payload](https://payloadspace.com/interstellar-lab-wins-deep-space-food-challenge/)) |
| Final test | 8 weeks at Ohio State University, run by 4 student "Simunauts" ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/centennial-challenges/meet-the-simunauts-ohio-state-students-to-test-space-food-solutions-for-nasa/)) |
| Mass, volume, power, yield | Not published |

In the final phase, judges scored food safety and acceptability, output compared with inputs (food quantity, nutrition and water use), reliability, and the stability of inputs and food ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/marshall/nasa-begins-final-phase-of-3-million-deep-space-food-challenge/)). Teams had to pass milestones for safety, sensory testing, palatability and harvest volume ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-1-25-million-to-three-teams-at-deep-space-food-finale/)).

## Limitations and open issues

- **No public specifications.** Mass, volume, power draw, water use and yields are not published, so its share of a crew's diet cannot be checked.
- **Short ground test only.** The longest public test was eight weeks on Earth ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/centennial-challenges/meet-the-simunauts-ohio-state-students-to-test-space-food-solutions-for-nasa/)), far short of a multi-year mission.
- **Not flown.** The only space-bound piece is the smaller Eden 1.0 research unit, waiting on Haven-1's launch ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haven-1)). Behaviour in 1/6 g and in lunar dust is untested.
- **Micronutrients, not calories.** Its outputs are greens, mushrooms and insects for micronutrients ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-1-25-million-to-three-teams-at-deep-space-food-finale/)); staple calories must come from other systems.
- **Small company risk.** Long-term support depends on one small firm; the automation level of 90% comes from press coverage, not a published test report.
