## What it is

The Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) is the set of machines that keeps the International Space Station's air and water fit for people. It controls cabin pressure, oxygen, ventilation, fire detection, waste and water supply. The regenerative part has three main pieces: the Water Recovery System, the Air Revitalization System and the Oxygen Generation System. They were designed and tested by NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center with industry partners ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss)). Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Honeywell and UTC Aerospace Systems (now Collins) all took part ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_ECLSS)).

The US oxygen generator became operational in July 2007. The Water Recovery System arrived on shuttle flight STS-126 in November 2008 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_ECLSS)). By April 2019 the oxygen generator had made more than 16,184 lb (about 7,340 kg) of oxygen ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190030425)). Russia runs its own parallel systems, Elektron for oxygen and Vozdukh for CO2 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_ECLSS)).

The headline number is water. NASA's reference page still says the system recovers "about 90 percent" of station water ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss)). In June 2023 NASA reported that a new Brine Processor Assembly lifted total recovery from 93-94% to 98%, the goal NASA sets for missions beyond low Earth orbit ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/); [Space.com](https://www.space.com/astronaut-pee-iss-water-recycling-98-percent-milestone)). NASA now uses the ISS as a testbed to mature this hardware for Moon and Mars missions ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240009073)).

## Why Open Moon uses it

Open Moon's first habitat (M08) uses ISS-derived life support for water recovery, oxygen generation and CO2 removal, topped up with water and oxygen from the M06 pilot plants. The M08 exit criteria require life support to run uncrewed for three months before the first crew arrives. M09 (permanent crew) then needs at least 90% water recovery averaged over a year and adds brine processing, electrolysis, a four-bed CO2 scrubber and a Sabatier reactor.

The reason is mass. NASA's lunar logistics study found that with fully open-loop life support, water and gases are about half of all logistics needs. Closing the oxygen and water loops could largely remove needs worth almost 40% of the total. Packaging and carriers add another 75-150% on top ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230016304)). Without recycling, every crew-year on the Moon would need far more cargo landings. ECLSS is the only regenerative life support with decades of crewed flight time, which is why Open Moon picks it over newer options.

## How it works

- **Water.** The Urine Processor Assembly boils urine at low pressure in a spinning distiller, since there is no gravity to separate liquid and gas ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_ECLSS)). Its water joins cabin condensate and other wastewater. The Water Processor Assembly then filters it, breaks down trace contaminants in a catalytic reactor, checks purity with sensors, adds iodine and stores it ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/)). The Brine Processor blows warm, dry air over leftover brine through a membrane. Cabin air systems then collect that water too ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/)).
- **Oxygen.** An electrolysis cell stack splits recovered water into oxygen for the cabin and hydrogen ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss)).
- **CO2 and trace gases.** Molecular sieve beds capture CO2. Charcoal, a catalytic oxidizer and a lithium hydroxide bed remove trace contaminants ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss)).
- **Oxygen recovery.** A Sabatier reactor combines waste hydrogen with crew CO2 to make water and methane, and the methane is vented ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss)). Venting that methane wastes hydrogen, which limits oxygen recovery from CO2 to about 50% ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190030371)).

## Limitations and open issues

- **Reliability.** The original CO2 remover (CDRA) has a long history of unplanned maintenance and obsolete parts ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205003542)). Its four-bed replacement has been removing CO2 on the ISS since October 2021 and is still being turned into a standard ISS system ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260007665)).
- **Past failures needed Earth.** The urine distiller failed one day after installation in 2008. In 2010 the US oxygen generator stopped working well, and the crew relied on Elektron and shipped oxygen for six months until a repair kit arrived ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_ECLSS)). A Moon base cannot count on fast resupply like this.
- **Built for a supply chain.** ISS hardware uses swappable units that are often returned to Earth for refurbishment. NASA says exploration missions will lack that supply chain and is only now testing component-level repair on the ISS ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240009073)).
- **Spares mass.** Regenerative systems cut water and gas resupply but generally add subsystem mass and spares ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230016304)).
- **Oxygen loop not closed.** The Sabatier step recovers only about half of the oxygen in CO2. Full hydrogen recovery (for example methane pyrolysis) is at brassboard stage ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190030371)).
- **Untested on the Moon.** NASA's exploration testbed mimics a Mars transit habitat in microgravity ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240009073)). No published test covers the hardware in lunar gravity or with lunar dust.
- **Food and nitrogen are not recycled.** The system handles air and water only. MELiSSA-type biological loops aim to close food too ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MELiSSA)).
- **Long-run 98%.** NASA reported 98% from assessments of the brine processor demonstration ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/)), while its reference page still quotes about 90% ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss)). Average year-round recovery is not published.
