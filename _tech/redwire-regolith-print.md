## What it is

Redwire Regolith Print (RRP) was a small technology demonstration on the International Space Station. It tested 3D printing with a feedstock made from simulated lunar soil. Redwire developed it with [NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-to-demonstrate-in-space-additive-manufacturing-for-lunar-surface-on-the-international-space-station). It ran on Redwire's Additive Manufacturing Facility (AMF), a commercial printer built by Made In Space. That printer was [installed on the ISS in April 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_In_Space). Redwire [bought Made In Space in June 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_In_Space).

The hardware flew on Northrop Grumman's 16th cargo mission. That mission [launched on an Antares rocket on 10 August 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_NG-16). Redwire says this was the [first time lunar regolith simulant was used for 3D printing in space](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-regolith-manufacturing-technology-wins-popular-science-2021-best-of-whats-new-award/). By December 2021, the printed samples had [returned to Earth for scientific analysis](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-regolith-manufacturing-technology-wins-popular-science-2021-best-of-whats-new-award/). The project won a [2021 Popular Science "Best of What's New" award](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-regolith-manufacturing-technology-wins-popular-science-2021-best-of-whats-new-award/).

NASA lists RRP among its ISS manufacturing studies. NASA says it [tested simulated regolith as a feedstock for 3D manufacturing in orbit](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/3d-printing-saving-weight-and-space-at-launch/). We could not find any published results from the returned samples, such as strength or density. This page does not give numbers for them.

## Why Open Moon uses it

Milestone M12 aims to make most new construction mass and many spare parts from lunar materials. RRP is the first regolith-based printing run in space that Open Moon could find, per [Redwire](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-regolith-manufacturing-technology-wins-popular-science-2021-best-of-whats-new-award/). Redwire framed the goal as building [landing pads, foundations, roads, habitats and furnishings](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-to-demonstrate-in-space-additive-manufacturing-for-lunar-surface-on-the-international-space-station) from local material, so that Artemis missions launch less mass.

In Open Moon, RRP is a pathfinder, not the main construction tool. It shows that a regolith-filled feedstock can be extruded in low gravity. That gives confidence for printing small regolith parts inside the base, such as tiles, brackets and furnishings. Large structures depend on bigger systems such as [ICON's Olympus](https://www.iconbuild.com/newsroom/icon-to-develop-lunar-surface-construction-system-with-57-2-million-nasa-award). Without this kind of work, every small non-critical part would still have to come from Earth.

## How it works

The AMF is a fused filament fabrication (FFF) printer. A heated nozzle lays down molten thermoplastic in layers on a moving bed. On the ISS it normally prints [ABS, ULTEM 9085 and HDPE](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190030402). For RRP, Redwire flew [three custom print heads and three print bed surfaces](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-to-demonstrate-in-space-additive-manufacturing-for-lunar-surface-on-the-international-space-station) designed for regolith-filled material, to fit the existing printer.

The feedstock was a [polymer binder mixed with the lunar simulant JSC-1A](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2021/07/how-3d-printing-moonrock-material-could-lead-new-space-habitats/183649/). NASA chose and supplied the simulant. Redwire called it a feedstock ["optimized for compressive strength"](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2021/07/how-3d-printing-moonrock-material-could-lead-new-space-habitats/183649/). JSC-1A is made from [a basaltic ash with high glass content, meant to resemble mare soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_regolith_simulant). The plan was [three prints, each a solid slab](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2021/07/how-3d-printing-moonrock-material-could-lead-new-space-habitats/183649/). These were to be returned to NASA scientists for testing.

## Limitations and open issues

- **No published results.** Redwire said the samples were [undergoing analysis](https://rdw.com/newsroom/redwire-regolith-manufacturing-technology-wins-popular-science-2021-best-of-whats-new-award/) in late 2021. We found no public strength or quality data since.
- **Wrong environment.** The ISS is microgravity inside a pressurised cabin. The Moon has one-sixth gravity, hard vacuum and extreme cold. Ground tests of a similar regolith-plastic process had to be run in [dirty thermal vacuum near -190 C](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230015024) to learn how it behaves there.
- **The binder comes from Earth.** The plastic share must be shipped. Work on [plastics that could be grown in space](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260003641) is only at the lab stage.
- **Simulant, not real soil.** JSC-1A imitates [mare soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_regolith_simulant). The south polar base sits in [highlands terrain](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250002845), which is chemically different.
- **Very small scale.** RRP planned [three slabs](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2021/07/how-3d-printing-moonrock-material-could-lead-new-space-habitats/183649/) in a station printer. Scaling to pads or walls is a different engineering problem.
