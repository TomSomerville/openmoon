## What it is

Starship HLS is the lunar version of SpaceX's Starship upper stage. It is one of NASA's two Human Landing Systems for Artemis. It stands about [52 m (171 ft) tall, uses six Raptor engines burning liquid methane and liquid oxygen, and is the second stage of the Super Heavy rocket](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf). It is [9 m wide, carries 2 to 4 crew, and has no heat shield or fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_HLS) because it never returns to Earth.

NASA chose SpaceX in 2021. The [Option A contract was worth about $3 billion and Option B about $1 billion, for a total of about $4.3 billion; by December 2025 it had grown by about $253 million](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf). The OIG found the Artemis III lander delayed by at least two years. NASA then changed the plan. [Artemis III (2027) is now an Earth-orbit docking test with a Version 3 Starship test article, and Artemis IV (2028) is the first crewed landing](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-shares-plans-to-accelerate-artemis-return-to-moon/).

Flight testing is moving fast. [Flight 14 (28 September 2026) reached orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_14) despite losing one engine. On [Flight 15 (13 November 2026) the ship spent more than 22 hours in orbit and was then caught by the tower, the first catch of a Starship upper stage from orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_15). NASA also plans a cargo variant. It says the [cargo versions of both crew landers will deliver about 15 t (33,000 lb) to the surface](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf).

## Why Open Moon uses it

Open Moon needs one heavy lander for its largest items: habitats, the fission reactor and large ISRU plants. Starship cargo is one of two candidates for the "heavy cargo" class in Open Moon's transport plan. The habitat in M08 and later propellant work in M11 depend on that class. NASA already intends to give SpaceX the job of [landing JAXA's pressurized rover no earlier than FY2032](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf). That makes Starship one of the first landers likely to carry a large payload to the Moon.

It is also a possible crew ride. Starship HLS has an [elevator that moves crew and cargo between the cabin and the surface](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/human-landing-systems/).

Without a heavy lander, every habitat and power unit would have to be split into pieces of about 3 t or less for landers like Blue Moon Mark 1. That means more landings, more assembly on the surface and more ways to fail.

## How it works

A Starship lander cannot reach the Moon on one tank. SpaceX first launches a **depot** ship to low Earth orbit. Then [more than 10 tanker flights fill it, one every 6 days, starting more than 200 days before crew launch](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf). The lander docks with the depot and refuels, then flies to lunar orbit. There it can [wait up to 100 days](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf). Near the surface it uses [thrusters on the mid-body, not the main Raptors, to limit plume damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_HLS).

## Limitations and open issues

- **Refuelling is unproven.** Moving cryogenic propellant [between two spacecraft has never been done](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250004625). Only a [tank-to-tank transfer inside one ship (Flight 3, March 2024)](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/artemis-campaign-development-division/human-landing-system-program/nasa-artemis-mission-progresses-with-spacex-starship-test-flight) has flown. The ship-to-ship test slipped from March 2025 and is now [listed as no earlier than Q1 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_Propellant_Transfer_Demonstration).
- **Launch cadence.** The OIG notes SpaceX has [not shown the required 12 to 24 day pad turnaround](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf) needed for the tanker campaign.
- **Schedule.** NASA's own plan puts the [first tanker flight and the uncrewed landing demo in late 2027 to early 2028](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf). The OIG said each failed test flight costs [an estimated 1 to 3 months](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf).
- **Cargo variant details.** No public cargo deck size, unloading method or price per landing has been published.
- **Tall lander.** Cargo sits many metres above the ground. Open Moon needs the elevator or a surface crane to unload it.
- **Plume effects.** A large lander landing near base hardware will throw regolith; NASA is still [testing plume-surface interaction](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf).
- **Booster recovery.** On Flight 15 the [booster's tower catch was called off and it was lost in the Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_15), so rapid reuse is still being proven.
