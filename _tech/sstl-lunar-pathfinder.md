## What it is

Lunar Pathfinder is a communications relay satellite for lunar orbit. It is built by Surrey Satellite Technology Ltd (SSTL) in the UK and is [ESA's first lunar communications relay satellite](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon), developed as a commercial partnership. It will pass data between Earth and spacecraft on and around the Moon. SSTL sells the service under its "SSTL Lunar" brand to both commercial and agency customers ([SSTL service guide](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf)). It is also the [first element of ESA's Moonlight](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon) communications and navigation service.

The project started in April 2018, when [ESA, SSTL and Goonhilly Earth Station signed a collaboration agreement](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/ESA_signs_collaboration_agreement_for_commercial_Lunar_missions) for commercial lunar services. By October 2019 it was in a [pilot phase](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/A_pathway_for_communicating_at_the_Moon). In March 2023 [Firefly Aerospace won the NASA CLPS task](https://sstl.co.uk/media-hub/latest-news/2023/firefly-to-take-lunar-pathfinder-to-the-moon) to carry it to lunar orbit. Under a [barter agreement](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon), NASA pays for the ride and gets access to the relay service in return.

The schedule has slipped several times. The 2022 service guide expected [launch at the end of Q4 2024](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf), and ESA's 2024 Moonlight page said [operations would begin in 2026](https://www.esa.int/articles/esa-s-moonlight-programme-pioneering-the-path-for-lunar-exploration). As of October 2026 the satellite was in its [final environmental test campaign in the UK](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon), with launch planned for 2027 on a Falcon 9 as part of [Blue Ghost Mission 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ghost_Mission_2). ESA says [ten international user missions](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon) already plan to use it.

## Why Open Moon uses it

Milestone M03 (communications and navigation) needs a reliable link to Earth. Open Moon's approach is to buy relay service rather than build a constellation. Lunar Pathfinder is one of the services on that list, next to NASA's commercial relay network and ESA Moonlight.

A relay matters because the base will not always see Earth. SSTL notes that [far-side assets cannot reach Earth at all without a relay](https://www.sstl.co.uk/what-we-do/lunar-mission-services), and polar sites may have only limited direct-to-Earth visibility because of terrain. A relay also lets rovers leave their lander's line of sight and keep working after the lander dies. Its orbit is designed to favour the [lunar south polar region](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf), where Open Moon plans to build.

A relay also eases the load on Earth's ground stations. NASA's Deep Space Network already sees [demand exceed supply by about 40% at times](https://oig.nasa.gov/docs/IG-23-016.pdf). Without relays, the early robotic missions would be tied to short direct-to-Earth windows, and far-side or shadowed sites would be out of reach. As the first relay due to arrive, Pathfinder is Open Moon's earliest chance of a second, independent Earth link.

## How it works

Pathfinder flies in an Elliptical Lunar Frozen Orbit (ELFO). This orbit stays stable over time and gives [long daily passes over the southern hemisphere](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf), including the far side. Firefly's [Elytra transfer vehicle](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon) will release it into this orbit.

User spacecraft talk to Pathfinder over short-range S-band or UHF links using the [CCSDS Proximity-1 protocol](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf). Pathfinder can run [one S-band and one UHF channel at the same time](https://www.sstl.co.uk/what-we-do/lunar-mission-services), then relays the data to Earth in X-band. Data rate adjusts during a pass as signal strength changes. [Ground stations in the guide](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf) include Goonhilly, an Indian DSN antenna and Kongsberg Satellite Services. Users can buy an autonomous daily data volume, scheduled contacts, or a priority emergency service.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Orbit (baseline) | [673 x 7,331 km altitude, 54.9° inclination, eccentricity 0.58](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf) |
| Moon links | S-band (2025-2110 MHz up, 2200-2290 MHz down) and UHF (390-405 / 435-450 MHz), 0.5 kbps to 2 Mbps depending on user |
| Earth link | X-band, up to 30 kbps uplink, up to 5 Mbps downlink |
| Design life | [About 8 years](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon) |
| Power | [Two deployable solar arrays and batteries](https://space.skyrocket.de/doc_sdat/lunar-pathfinder.htm) |
| Mass | No current figure published |

It also carries [four experiments](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Lunar_Pathfinder_Europe_s_first_step_towards_a_connected_Moon): the RADMON radiation monitor, a GNSS receiver to test GPS/Galileo use at the Moon, a NASA laser retroreflector and an X-band ranging transponder. A JPL User Terminal landing on Blue Ghost will be used to [commission the relay](https://sstl.co.uk/media-hub/latest-news/2023/firefly-to-take-lunar-pathfinder-to-the-moon), and NASA's LuSEE-Night radio telescope will be one of its first users.

## Limitations and open issues

- **Not yet flown.** It is still in testing. Launch has slipped from late 2024 to 2027, and it now depends on Blue Ghost Mission 2 and the Elytra vehicle working.
- **One satellite only.** In the northern hemisphere, some sites will see [gaps in coverage of several days](https://www.sstl.co.uk/getmedia/edaaa697-405c-458d-8394-97f83130d54a/Lunar-Pathfinder-Service-Guide-V2-2.pdf) on a 28-day cycle. There is no spare if it fails.
- **Modest capacity.** The Earth downlink is at most 5 Mbps and is shared by all users. That is enough for robots, not for live HD video from a crewed base.
- **Published figures differ.** The 2022 guide lists S-band and UHF links, while an [ESA partner page describes two S-band links](https://bsgn.esa.int/service/lunar-pathfinder/). The orbit values are marked as indicative. Final numbers should be confirmed with SSTL.
- **Pricing is not public.** ESA lists the starting price as ["upon request"](https://bsgn.esa.int/service/lunar-pathfinder/).
- **Interoperability.** Open Moon radios must speak Proximity-1 on Pathfinder's frequencies. How it fits the shared [LunaNet standard](https://www.nasa.gov/communicating-with-missions/scan/lcrns/lunanet-interoperability-specification/) with Moonlight and NASA relays still has to be shown in orbit.
