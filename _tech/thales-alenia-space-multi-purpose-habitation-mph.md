## What it is

The Multi-Purpose Habitation (MPH) module is a rigid, pressurised habitat for the lunar surface. The Italian Space Agency (ASI) leads it, and Thales Alenia Space in Turin is the prime contractor. [Thales Alenia Space](https://www.thalesaleniaspace.com/en/press-releases/thales-alenia-space-signs-contract-italian-space-agency-asi-develop-first-human) calls it the first dedicated habitation asset on the Moon. It is planned to launch from NASA's Kennedy Space Center in 2033 and is designed for at least 10 years of operation. Thales Alenia Space also builds pressurised modules for Gateway: ESPRIT and I-HAB for ESA, and HALO for Northrop Grumman ([Thales Alenia Space](https://www.thalesaleniaspace.com/en/news/lunar-multi-purpose-habitat-activities-officially-underway)).

The project grew out of a June 2022 ASI–NASA agreement that gave ASI the preliminary design of the module ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/asi-awards-new-lunar-habitat-contract-to-thales-alenia-space/)). NASA's Element Initiation Review finished in October 2023 ([Thales Alenia Space](https://www.thalesaleniaspace.com/en/news/lunar-multi-purpose-habitat-activities-officially-underway)). ASI's Mission Definition Review followed in July 2024 ([Media INAF](https://www.media.inaf.it/2024/07/25/programma-artemis-modulo-mph/)). A NASA Mission Concept Review board approved the next phase in September 2024 ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/nasa-greenlights-next-phase-of-italian-lunar-habitat-project/)). In July 2025 ASI signed a two-year preliminary design contract with Thales Alenia Space, working with Altec ([Thales Alenia Space](https://www.thalesaleniaspace.com/en/press-releases/thales-alenia-space-signs-contract-italian-space-agency-asi-develop-first-human)).

In March 2026 NASA paused Gateway to focus on a surface base, and Italy signed a Statement of Intent with NASA that names habitation modules as part of its contribution. On 19 May 2026 a NASA board completed a combined System Definition and System Requirements Review, clearing the way to a Preliminary Design Review in 2027 ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/italys-lunar-habitat-clears-nasa-system-requirements-review/)). In September 2026 NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said Italy is committing over $5 billion to the habitat and will get two astronaut seats on the surface. The module is expected to be ready in 2032 ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/italy-to-spend-over-5-billion-to-send-two-astronauts-to-the-moon/)).

## Why Open Moon uses it

The MPH is the reference habitat for milestone M08, "First habitat and crewed sorties". It is the place where crews live during the first 14–30-day stays while they commission power, ISRU and mobility systems. Its mass of about 15 t matches what NASA says the cargo versions of the crewed landers can deliver: about 15 t. NASA plans to award delivery of a lunar surface habitat no earlier than FY2033 ([NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf)). That fits NASA's Moon Base Phase Three (2032 onward), when habitats are assembled into a permanent outpost ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/)).

It is the only lunar surface habitat with a funded design contract and NASA review milestones behind it. Without it, M08 would rely on concepts such as NASA's own Surface Habitat study ([NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220000245)), or on inflatable modules not yet designed for the Moon. Crewed sorties would then slip, and so would the later milestones that depend on people being on site.

## How it works

The MPH is a cylinder about 3 m wide and 6 m long. It sits on wheels so it can be positioned precisely and serve crews at different sites ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/asi-awards-new-lunar-habitat-contract-to-thales-alenia-space/)). Between crews it runs science experiments on its own ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/nasa-greenlights-next-phase-of-italian-lunar-habitat-project/)). Because it stays on the surface for good, it needs its own power generation and storage, thermal and environmental control, and communications. The design mixes heritage hardware with new technology ([IAC-24 paper](https://iafastro.directory/iac/paper/id/86708/abstract-pdf/IAC-24,B3,1,2,x86708.brief.pdf)). Early work focuses on the big hazards: thermal swings, dust, radiation, micrometeoroids and low gravity ([Thales Alenia Space](https://www.thalesaleniaspace.com/en/press-releases/thales-alenia-space-signs-contract-italian-space-agency-asi-develop-first-human)). Altec in Turin will run ground control ([Media INAF](https://www.media.inaf.it/2024/07/25/programma-artemis-modulo-mph/)).

## Limitations and open issues

- **Early design stage.** The Preliminary Design Review is not due until 2027 ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/italys-lunar-habitat-clears-nasa-system-requirements-review/)). No flight hardware exists, and the 2033 launch leaves little margin.
- **Small crew.** It is sized for two astronauts on one 7–30-day mission a year ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/asi-awards-new-lunar-habitat-contract-to-thales-alenia-space/)). NASA's Surface Habitat concept was for up to four crew for 30–60 days ([NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220000245)).
- **Design may change.** Wheeled mobility could be revisited as NASA moves to a more fixed Moon Base layout ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/italy-to-spend-over-5-billion-to-send-two-astronauts-to-the-moon/)).
- **Hard environment.** Dust on mechanisms, lunar gravity effects and the cold lunar night are new problems compared with orbital modules ([IAC-24 paper](https://iafastro.directory/iac/paper/id/86708/abstract-pdf/IAC-24,B3,1,2,x86708.brief.pdf)).
- **Depends on a heavy lander.** Delivery needs a ~15 t cargo lander that has not flown yet ([NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf)).
- **Funding not fully formalised.** The $5 billion figure comes from a speech, and in September 2026 a new agreement on Italy's MPH contribution was still to be signed ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/italy-to-spend-over-5-billion-to-send-two-astronauts-to-the-moon/)).
