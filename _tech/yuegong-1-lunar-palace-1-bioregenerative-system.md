## What it is

Yuegong-1, or Lunar Palace 1, is a sealed test habitat in Beijing. It was built by Beihang University (formerly BUAA), with a lab designed by Professor Liu Hong. Construction began in March 2013 and it opened in January 2014 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Palace_1)). It is a bioregenerative life support system (BLSS): people, crop plants, mealworms and microbes recycle oxygen, water and food between them ([Beihang](https://ev.buaa.edu.cn/info/1022/3019.htm)).

The first test, in 2014, kept three crew inside for 105 days. Oxygen and water were recycled and 55% of the food was grown inside ([Fu et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27912029/)). Beihang gives the material closure of that test as 97% ([Beihang](https://ev.buaa.edu.cn/info/1022/3019.htm)).

The facility was then upgraded for "Lunar Palace 365". It ran 370 days, from 10 May 2017 to 15 May 2018 ([ECNS](http://www.ecns.cn/2018/05-15/302681.shtml)). Eight student volunteers worked in two groups of four, in three phases: 60, 200 and 105 days. Material closure reached 98.2% ([Fu et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1)). The full results were published in a peer-reviewed journal in 2024 ([Fu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2024.12.002)). It has not flown in space. Beihang says the team plans experiments in space station and lunar environments ([Beihang](https://ev.buaa.edu.cn/info/1022/3019.htm)).

## Why Open Moon uses it

Food is the hardest supply to close. NASA's reference diet is 12.8 MJ and 0.8 kg of food solids per person per day ([NASA BVAD](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210024855)). Machines on the ISS already recover 98% of water ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/)), but they grow no food.

Lunar Palace 1 is the best evidence that a small crew can live for a year inside a loop that also makes food. It is Open Moon's reference design for closed-loop life support with crops in milestone M10 (Food, phase 4). Without it, the base would depend on food shipped from Earth for as long as people live there. It would also lack a tested example of how crops, waste treatment and crew can be run together.

## How it works

The habitat has one crew cabin and two plant cabins ([ECNS](http://www.ecns.cn/2018/05-15/302681.shtml)). The system links crop growing, animal protein production, urine nitrogen recycling and conversion of solid waste ([Fu et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27912029/)). Plants regenerate the oxygen. Mealworms eat leftover and inedible plant parts and supply protein ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Palace_1)).

The 2014 test grew 21 plant species ([Fu et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27912029/)), including wheat as the main calorie and oxygen source, plus vegetables and strawberries ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Palace_1)). In Lunar Palace 365, O2, CO2 and trace gases stayed in safe ranges. The system recovered quickly from disturbances such as crew changes ([Fu et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1)).

## Limitations and open issues

- **Ground only.** All tests ran in Earth gravity, under Earth's atmosphere and radiation shielding. Partial gravity, lunar dust and radiation have not been tested.
- **Not fully closed.** At 98.2% closure, some mass still had to come in from outside. Only 67% of solid waste was recovered ([Fu et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1)). Plant food was fully grown inside, but about 20% of total food still came from outside ([ECNS](http://www.ecns.cn/2018/05-15/302681.shtml)).
- **Power and mass not public.** We found no published figures for lighting power, total mass or flight-ready volume. These drive the cost of a lunar version.
- **Small crew.** At most four people lived inside at once. Scaling to a larger base is unproven.
- **Biological risk.** Microbe levels stayed low and safe across 370 days ([Fu et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40555951/)). But Biosphere 2 showed that large closed systems can drift: oxygen fell from 20.9% to 14.5% and food ran short ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_2)).
- **Crew psychology.** Long isolation affects mood. The team has studied links between gut microbes and mood ([Hao et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37095530/)), but this remains a risk.
- **Access.** This is a Chinese university facility. Its detailed design data are not openly available for Open Moon to reuse.
