# Water ice at the lunar south pole: evidence, location, amount

Researched 18 Nov 2026. Every claim below links to a source that was opened during this research. Nothing is estimated. Where a value is not published, the text says so. Numbers in square brackets point to the [Sources](#sources) list.

## Summary

- **Water ice is confirmed in south polar cold traps, but only in small amounts and in patches.** LCROSS hit the Cabeus PSR in 2009 and found 5.6 ± 2.9 wt% water in the ejected regolith ([AAAS/Science, Colaprete et al. 2010](https://www.aaas.org/news/science-lcross-impact-ejects-minerals-and-frozen-water-crater-moon) [1]). M3 detected water-ice spectra at the optical surface (top few mm). Only about 3.5% of cold traps showed ice exposures ([Li et al. 2018, PNAS](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6130389) [4]).
- **The newest imaging finds little surface ice.** ShadowCam (2026) found "no evidence of widespread water ice in PSRs at abundances above the detection limit of 20 to 30 wt %". It could not rule out lower concentrations. A few small spots could be >10 wt% ice ([Li et al. 2026, Science Advances](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12998505) [5]).
- **Most of the ice is probably buried.** LEND sees hydrogen in the top ~1 m, with a peak of 0.54 wt% water at Cabeus ([NASA LEND summary of Sanin et al. 2017](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/NuggetHydrogenAtTheLunarPoles20170210.pdf) [7]). An ISRO-led study puts subsurface ice in the top couple of metres at 5–8× the surface amount ([ISRO, Apr 2024](https://www.isro.gov.in/Recent_studies_suggest_more_ice_Moon.html) [10]). Chandrayaan-2 radar shows possible ice under four doubly shadowed craters ([ISRO, 27 May 2026](https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan2_Dual_Frequency_Synthetic_Aperture_Radar.html) [9]).
- **The evidence for Shackleton is mixed.** Kaguya saw no exposed, fairly pure ice on the floor ([Haruyama et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1164020) [12]). LOLA brightness allows up to 22% ice, but only in a micron-thick layer ([MIT News, 2012](https://news.mit.edu/2012/shackletons-crater-0620) [13]). Mini-RF sets an upper limit of ~5–10 wt% in the top metre of the walls ([Thomson et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012GL052119) [14]). M3 ice detections cluster near Shackleton ([Li et al. 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6130389) [4]).
- **de Gerlache has two candidate surface-ice detections.** One is an M3 ice-bearing pixel example ([Li et al. 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6130389) [4]). The other is an unexplained bright spot that ShadowCam imaged at 88.5352°S, 97.4007°W ([Li et al. 2026](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12998505) [5]). Neither is confirmed.
- **Spudis crater and the small ridge craters have no published ice measurements.** LROC's ridge map says only that the ridge craters "host permanently shadowed regions, which may harbor volatiles" ([LROC ridge maps, Mar 2024](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/data/support/popular_downloads/LROC_Shackleton_deGerlache_Ridge_maps.zip) [16]).
- **Cold traps below 110 K cover about 13,000 km² poleward of 80°S** in Diviner data ([Williams et al. 2019, cited in Hayne et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05369) [18]). Hayne et al. 2021, counting cold traps at all scales down to ~1 cm, estimate about 23,000 km² for the south ([Hayne et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05369) [18]).
- **No lander has measured polar ice in place yet.** PRIME-1 on IM-2 tipped over and never drilled ([NASA NTRS, Apr 2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260003150/downloads/MSolo%20PRIME-1%20overview%20and%20results%20-%20LSSW.pdf) [20]). Chang'e-7 did not launch in its Aug 2026 window and is now listed for 2027 ([CGTN, 25 Aug 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-25/Explainer-Why-Chang-e-7-can-t-simply-launch-a-few-days-later-1PT8y06o38A/p.html) [22]; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_7) [23]).

## Our focus area

### Shackleton (21 km, at the pole)

- **Shadow and setting:** The interior is a PSR. The crater is 21 km across and over 4 km deep ([LROC "Islands in the Dark"](https://www.lroc.asu.edu/images/1105) [27]).
- **For ice:**
  - Clementine radar suggested ice ([JAXA](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2008/10/20081024_kaguya_e.html) [11]).
  - LOLA shows a bright floor, consistent with ≤22% ice in a micron-thick layer ([MIT](https://news.mit.edu/2012/shackletons-crater-0620) [13]).
  - Mini-RF allows up to ~5–10 wt% in the top metre of the walls ([Thomson et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012GL052119) [14]).
  - M3 ice detections in the south cluster near Shackleton ([Li et al. 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6130389) [4]).
- **Against, or not conclusive:**
  - Kaguya found no exposed, relatively pure ice on the floor ([Haruyama et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1164020) [12]).
  - Mini-RF's own preferred explanation is roughness ([Thomson et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012GL052119) [14]).
  - The LOLA team attributes the bright walls possibly to fresh exposed material ([MIT](https://news.mit.edu/2012/shackletons-crater-0620) [13]). Fisher et al. say Shackleton's brightness is "likely largely due to its steep walls promoting mass wasting" ([Fisher et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170003782/downloads/20170003782.pdf) [6]).
  - ShadowCam's 2026 paper shows bright boulders on Shackleton's PSR floor as an example of bright features that are not ice ([Li et al. 2026](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12998505) [5]).
  - ShadowCam's first image shows part of the floor rising above 110 K in summer ([ShadowCam](https://www.shadowcam.asu.edu/images/1284) [19]).
- **Temperature disagreement:** Kaguya estimated the floor at < ~90 K ([Haruyama et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1164020) [12]). The ShadowCam team says part of the floor exceeds 110 K in summer ([ShadowCam](https://www.shadowcam.asu.edu/images/1284) [19]). No published Diviner map of temperatures across the whole floor was found in this research.
- **Observing from the rim:** LROC marks a flat spot where the ridge meets Shackleton's rim (89.684°S, 196.739°E area). From there a landed asset would have "a direct line of sight onto the Shackleton crater floor" and could observe the PSR by light reflected from the inner wall ([LROC "Traversing the Shackleton de Gerlache Ridge"](https://www.lroc.asu.edu/images/1247) [28]).

### de Gerlache (~89.0°S)

- **M3:** One of the three example ice spectra in Li et al. 2018 comes from a possible ice-bearing pixel in "the crater De Gerlache in the south pole" ([Li et al. 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6130389) [4]).
- **ShadowCam:** A "possible high-reflectance anomaly" at **88.5352°S, 97.4007°W** inside the de Gerlache PSR. It does not change with lighting, and it is not a fresh crater. The authors list it as a candidate for surface ice "or other types of volatile ice and materials with high reflectance". They ask for observations at more phase angles to test it ([Li et al. 2026](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12998505) [5]). It is unconfirmed.
- **Slope ice:** A 2022 Icarus paper is titled "Characteristics of de Gerlache crater, site of girlands and slope exposed ice in a lunar polar depression" (Kereszturi et al., [DOI 10.1016/j.icarus.2022.115231](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2022.115231) [29]). The full text could not be retrieved (publisher returned 403), so its findings are not summarised here.
- **Amount:** No published wt% or mass value specific to de Gerlache was found.

### Spudis (13 km, centred 89.51°S, 84.61°W)

- Spudis was adopted by the IAU on 30 Sep 2021. It spans 89.30–89.73°S and 65.09–107.79°W ([USGS Gazetteer](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/Feature/16007) [30]).
- **No published water-ice measurement or PSR temperature specific to Spudis was found.** It is not named in the M3 (2018), ShadowCam (2026) or Chandrayaan-2 (2026) results reviewed here.

### Shackleton–de Gerlache ridge and its small craters

- LROC's map set, Sheet 1 (25 Mar 2024), says the ridge is a "30-kilometer-long topographic high". It "dog-legs around Vincent crater up towards de Gerlache crater". It also says: "Small craters (Worsley, Vincent, Holness, Marston, Wild, Cheetham, Wordie, McCarthy, Kerr, Hurley) along the ridge host permanently shadowed regions, which may harbor volatiles, such as water" ([LROC ridge maps](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/data/support/popular_downloads/LROC_Shackleton_deGerlache_Ridge_maps.zip) [16]).
- **These names are unofficial.** The same sheet lists only Shackleton, de Gerlache and Spudis as IAU-approved. It describes all the others as "unofficial nomenclature given by exploration teams, scientific groups, or astronauts" ([LROC, Sheet 1](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/data/support/popular_downloads/LROC_Shackleton_deGerlache_Ridge_maps.zip) [16]). A search of IAU and USGS sources found no adopted lunar features with these names.
- **No published ice detection, concentration, temperature or coordinate list was found for any of these ten craters.** Whether they hold ice is unknown. For scale, the ShadowCam authors note that M3 pixels are 280 m, LOLA albedo pixels 500 m and LAMP pixels 240 m, which limits checks of features a few tens of metres across ([Li et al. 2026](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12998505) [5]).
- Hayne et al. 2021 model ~2,500 km² of cold-trapping area in shadows smaller than 100 m across the Moon. This shows small shadows can be cold traps in general, but the paper gives no figures for these craters ([Hayne et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05369) [18]).

**Where the sources disagree:**

- **Surface ice.** Orbital surface measurements disagree. M3 suggests some pixels hold ~30 wt% ([4]), and LOLA albedo allows up to 22% in a micron layer at Shackleton ([13]). ShadowCam finds no widespread ice above 20–30 wt% ([5]), and Kaguya saw no exposed relatively pure ice in Shackleton ([12]). The ShadowCam authors say the patchiness seen by LAMP and M3 may be "a true representation of its distribution or an artifact" of their low signal-to-noise and coverage ([5]).
- **What the data agree on.** All published values are small (≤ a few wt%), or patchy, or both. They differ in which depth they sense: the top few mm (M3), a micron (LOLA), the top ~1 m (neutrons, radar), or several metres (LCROSS, DFSAR).

## Temperatures and cold traps

- **Threshold.** Water ice is treated as stable at the surface where the annual maximum temperature is below ~110 K. M3 ice exposures coincide with temperatures < 110 K ([Li et al. 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6130389) [4]). The LOLA reflectance increase also sets in near 110 K ([Fisher et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170003782/downloads/20170003782.pdf) [6]).
- **Cold traps without ice.** Not all cold traps show ice. M3 found none in the cold traps of Amundsen, Hedervari, Idel'son L and Wiechert near the south pole ([Li et al. 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6130389) [4]).
- **Coldest measured.** The LCROSS impact site in Cabeus has an estimated subsurface temperature of 38 K ([Paige et al. 2010](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20966246) [17]). ISRO gives ~25 K for doubly shadowed craters ([ISRO 2026](https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan2_Dual_Frequency_Synthetic_Aperture_Radar.html) [9]).
- **Cold-trap areas:**
  - About 13,000 km² of cold traps lie poleward of 80°S at a 110 K Diviner threshold, including seasonal effects (Williams et al. 2019, as cited by [Hayne et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05369) [18]).
  - Hayne et al. 2021 give ~23,000 km² for the south once cold traps at all scales are included (north ~17,000 km²; Moon total ~40,000 km²). About 2,500 km² of that is in shadows < 100 m across ([Hayne et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05369) [18]).
  - The area below 110 K grows by a factor of 2.8 in southern winter (Williams et al. 2019, [JGR Planets, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JE006028) [31]; factor as reported in search results of the abstract).
- **Shackleton.** Estimated at < ~90 K ([Haruyama et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1164020) [12]). However, part of the floor exceeds 110 K in summer ([ShadowCam](https://www.shadowcam.asu.edu/images/1284) [19]).
- **No published temperatures were found for de Gerlache, Spudis, or the ridge craters.** LROC's PSR-resource ranking maps are overlaid on Diviner annual maximum temperature ([LROC "Mysteries Remain Frozen"](https://www.lroc.asu.edu/images/1255) [32]; Brown et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2021.114874)). The paper itself was not accessible, so its per-crater values are not reported here.

## Open questions and upcoming measurements

**Open questions, as stated by the sources:**

- Is the patchy, low-abundance surface ice real, or an artifact of low signal-to-noise data ([Li et al. 2026](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12998505) [5])? Missions with detection limits < 1 wt% are needed to test this ([5]).
- How much ice is buried, and how deep? ISRO calls drilling "primordial" for future missions ([ISRO 2024](https://www.isro.gov.in/Recent_studies_suggest_more_ice_Moon.html) [10]).
- Are the de Gerlache bright spot and other high-reflectance anomalies ice? They need multi-phase-angle ShadowCam imaging ([5]).
- Is the hydrogen seen by neutron instruments really water ice? JAXA noted in 2008 that it "was unknown that the possible hydrogen was from water-ice" ([JAXA](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2008/10/20081024_kaguya_e.html) [11]). Lunar Prospector's own estimate assumed the hydrogen is water ice ([NASA 1998](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/text/lp_pr_980305.txt) [2]).
