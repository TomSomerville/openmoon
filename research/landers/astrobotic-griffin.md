## Landing slope tolerance

- **No formal design or certified maximum landing slope is published.** None of these give a landing-slope limit in degrees: Astrobotic's [Griffin product page](https://www.astrobotic.com/lunar-delivery/landers/griffin-lander/), the [Payload User's Guide (PUG) v5.0, Aug 2021](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/astrobotic-lunar-landers-pug.pdf), NASA's [mission page](https://www.nasa.gov/event/clps-flight-astrobotics-griffin-mission-one/) and [JPL testing article](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/moon-base/nasa-jpl-testing-readies-voyager-technologies-lander-for-moon-mission/), or [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Mission_One). Searches of NTRS and NASA for Griffin and VIPER landing-slope requirements also found no lander figure.
- **Closest official statement (company, qualitative).** Voyager Technologies, which is acquiring Astrobotic ([SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/astrobotic-unveils-griffin-moon-base-ii-lander/)), wrote: "The Griffin lander's wide stance can easily tolerate a 10-degree slope, but that could still be too steep for its payloads to optimally perform their tasks." The page metadata dates it 22 Oct 2026 ([Voyager: How Voyager Chose Griffin-1's Lunar Landing Site](https://voyagertechnologies.com/insights/how-voyager-chose-griffins-lunar-landing-site/)). The post presents 10° as a slope the lander handles easily, not as its maximum.
- **Site-selection criterion (applies to both landers).** The PUG v5.0, under "Effective Slopes", says: "The effective slope takes into account natural slopes in the topography and the presence of rocks. Sites are typically selected with an effective slope ≤10°. Sites with a maximum expected rock height of 0.3 m are preferred." ([PUG p. 51](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/astrobotic-lunar-landers-pug.pdf)). This is a site-selection practice, not a lander limit.
- **Hazard detection (design spec).** Griffin has a "15 cm hazard detection threshold", and an optional scanning LiDAR can "detect and avoid slopes, rocks, craters" ([Astrobotic](https://www.astrobotic.com/lunar-delivery/landers/griffin-lander/)).
- **Not a landing figure.** The egress ramps were tested at up to 33° with a VIPER prototype ([Astrobotic](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotics-griffin-lander-and-nasas-viper-moon-rover-complete-complex-test-drives/)). That is a ramp angle for driving off, not a slope the lander can land on.
- **Demonstrated:** none. Griffin has not landed.

## Payload capacity

The figures differ by date and by type. Some are a lander's capacity and some are what one mission will carry.

| Figure | Type | Source (date) |
|---|---|---|
| 400 kg | Early product-line capacity | [Astrobotic: "Astrobotic Awarded NASA Study Contract for Polar Mission to the Moon"](https://www.astrobotic.com/?p=122) (18 Jun 2019): "Griffin is a lunar lander product line that will carry 400 kg of uncrewed payload to the Moon." |
| 500 kg | Capacity at the VIPER award | [Astrobotic: "Astrobotic Awarded $199.5 Million Contract to Deliver NASA Moon Rover"](https://www.astrobotic.com/astrobotic-awarded-199-5-million-contract-to-deliver-nasa-moon-rover/) (11 Jun 2020): Griffin "is capable of delivering up to 500 kg of mass to the lunar surface." |
| 625 kg | Design capacity, polar configuration | [Astrobotic PUG v5.0](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/astrobotic-lunar-landers-pug.pdf) (Aug 2021), p. 24: "POLAR – Lunar Surface Payload Delivery Capacity 625 kg; Surface Operations Duration Up to 14 Days". |
| 625 kg | Current design capacity | [Astrobotic Griffin page](https://www.astrobotic.com/lunar-delivery/landers/griffin-lander/) (accessed Nov 2026): "625 kg payload mass capacity". Also [SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/astrobotic-unveils-griffin-moon-base-ii-lander/) (15 Jun 2026): "Griffin can put 625 kilograms (about 1,400 pounds) of payload onto the lunar surface." |
| "more than 1,100 pounds (455 kilograms)" | Planned Griffin-1 cargo (one mission, not a capacity) | [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/moon-base/nasa-jpl-testing-readies-voyager-technologies-lander-for-moon-mission/) (13 Oct 2026). The [NASA event page](https://www.nasa.gov/event/clps-flight-astrobotics-griffin-mission-one/) says "more than 1,100 pounds of cargo". The pound and kilogram figures are quoted as NASA printed them. |
| "over 445 kg" | Planned Griffin-1 cargo (Wikipedia) | [Wikipedia: Griffin Mission One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Mission_One) (accessed Nov 2026). It cites the NASA article above, which says 455 kg, so this looks like a transcription difference. |

**Configurations.** The PUG's polar vs mid-latitude split applies to **Peregrine**, not Griffin: Peregrine is rated 70–90 kg mid-latitude (192 h) and 100 kg polar (192+ h) ([PUG pp. 13–14](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/astrobotic-lunar-landers-pug.pdf)). For Griffin the PUG gives only the **polar configuration (625 kg)**, as built for Griffin Mission One. It says "Additional configurations for lunar orbit, equatorial, far side, and many more delivery locations are possible. Contact Astrobotic for more details" (p. 23). **No Griffin mid-latitude or equatorial payload figure is published.**

**How the figures fit together.** In Astrobotic's own sources the stated capacity grew from 400 kg (2019) to 500 kg (2020) to 625 kg (PUG 2021, and still current). The 625 kg figure is the latest official one. The 445/455 kg figures describe what Griffin-1 will carry, not what Griffin can carry. Astrolab's FLIP rover on that mission is listed at 500 kg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Mission_One), citing [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/astrolab-to-fly-its-flip-lunar-lander-on-astrobotics-griffin-1-lander/)).

**Demonstrated:** none. Griffin has not flown.

## Landing record

- **Griffin:** 0 landing attempts as of 18 Nov 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Mission_One)).
- **Related lander:** Astrobotic's smaller Peregrine (Peregrine Mission One, Jan 2024) had a propellant leak and never attempted a landing ([Wikipedia: Peregrine Mission One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_Mission_One)). Peregrine is a different lander, so this is not a Griffin attempt.
- **Status (Nov 2026):**
  - NASA named the mission Moon Base II on 26 May 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Mission_One)).
  - The lander was unveiled on 15 Jun 2026, when Astrobotic said launch would be in Q4 2026 ([SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/astrobotic-unveils-griffin-moon-base-ii-lander/)).
  - It completed environmental testing at JPL, reported 13 Oct 2026 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/moon-base/nasa-jpl-testing-readies-voyager-technologies-lander-for-moon-mission/)).
  - Voyager now says the mission "recently completed acceptance testing and is on track to launch to the lunar South Pole in January 2027" ([Voyager Technologies](https://voyagertechnologies.com/insights/delivering-the-moon-base/)).
  - Launch vehicle is a SpaceX Falcon Heavy, from LC-39A according to Wikipedia. The landing site is Nobile Crater near the south pole ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/event/clps-flight-astrobotics-griffin-mission-one/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Mission_One)).
  - **Conflict:** NASA's event page, last updated 14 Oct 2026, still says "Late 2026" ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/event/clps-flight-astrobotics-griffin-mission-one/)).
