## Landing slope tolerance

**Not published as a lander design tolerance.** Astrobotic's guides give only a landing-site selection criterion, not a certified maximum slope for touchdown stability.

- **PUG v5.0 (August 2021):** this is the version Astrobotic currently links from its Peregrine page. It is also hosted by NASA at [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/astrobotic-lunar-landers-pug.pdf). Its "Landing Site – Effective Slopes" section says: "The effective slope takes into account natural slopes in the topography and the presence of rocks. Sites are typically selected with an effective slope ≤10°. Sites with a maximum expected rock height of 0.3 m are preferred." ([PUG v5.0, p. 51](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf)). This text covers both Peregrine and Griffin.
- **Peregrine PUG v3.0 (October 2018):** this version describes the Mission One (Lacus Mortis) site. It says: "Effective Slopes: ≤ 12° … The effective slope takes into account natural slopes in the topography and the presence of rocks. The greatest expected rock height is 0.35 m." ([PUG v3.0, p. 27](https://explorers.larc.nasa.gov/2019APSMEX/MO/pdf_files/Astrobotic%20-%20Payload%20User%20Guide%20v3%202018-10.pdf))
- **Conflicting figures:** the 2018 guide gives ≤12° (site-specific, M1 at Lacus Mortis). The 2021 guide gives ≤10° (typical site selection).
- **Related touchdown limits in PUG v3.0, p. 17:** maximum horizontal landing velocity 0.5 m/s and maximum vertical landing velocity 2.0 m/s.
- **Astrobotic Peregrine product page:** a scanning LiDAR "can also be added to detect and avoid slopes, rocks, craters" but the page gives no slope number ([Peregrine page](https://www.astrobotic.com/lunar-delivery/landers/peregrine-lander/)).
- **Demonstrated:** none. Peregrine never attempted a landing.
- **Searched:** both PUG versions ("slope", "tilt", "touchdown", "leg"), the Astrobotic Peregrine page, the NASA PM1 press kit and the PM1 Post-Mission Report. No lander-level maximum slope or tilt tolerance appears in any of them.

## Payload capacity

These are design specs. The capacity was never demonstrated on the surface.

|---|---|---|
| 90 kg "Payload Mass Capacity" | Current Astrobotic Peregrine page (destination not stated on the page) | [Peregrine page](https://www.astrobotic.com/lunar-delivery/landers/peregrine-lander/) |
| 70–90 kg | "Lunar Surface Payload Delivery Capacity", mid-latitude configuration, 192 h surface ops | [PUG v5.0, p. 13](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf) |
| 100 kg | "Lunar Surface Payload Delivery Capacity", polar configuration (side-mounted solar panels, stretched tanks) | [PUG v5.0, p. 14](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf) |
| 35 kg | Mission One non-NASA surface payload (2018 plan) | [PUG v3.0](https://explorers.larc.nasa.gov/2019APSMEX/MO/pdf_files/Astrobotic%20-%20Payload%20User%20Guide%20v3%202018-10.pdf) |
| 265 kg | "Maximum Lunar Delivery Capacity"; "for future missions, the entire structure is scalable to accommodate up to 265 kg" (2018) | [PUG v3.0](https://explorers.larc.nasa.gov/2019APSMEX/MO/pdf_files/Astrobotic%20-%20Payload%20User%20Guide%20v3%202018-10.pdf) |
| 90 kg | "maximum payload mass of 90 kg (200 lb) during Mission One" (wiki) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_Mission_One) |

The most recent official figure is 90 kg, or 100 kg in the polar configuration (PUG v5.0, 2021). The 265 kg figure in the 2018 guide was a projection for a scaled-up lander and does not appear in the 2021 guide. PUG v3.0 lists a lander wet mass of 1,283 kg. For what flew, PM1 carried 20 payloads (5 NASA, 15 from other organizations) per the [NASA press kit](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/np-2023-12-017-jsc-clps-astrobotic-press-kit-update-508.pdf). Total flown payload mass is not published in the sources found.

## Landing record

**Peregrine Mission One (PM1). The only flight. No landing attempt.**

- **Launch:** 8 Jan 2024, 2:18 am ET, on the maiden flight of ULA Vulcan from Cape Canaveral ([PM1 Post-Mission Report](https://astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PM1_Post-Mission-Report_2024-1.pdf)).
- **Planned landing site:** Sinus Viscositatis, near the Gruithuisen Domes ([NASA press kit](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/np-2023-12-017-jsc-clps-astrobotic-press-kit-update-508.pdf)). The 2018 plan was Lacus Mortis ([PUG v3.0](https://explorers.larc.nasa.gov/2019APSMEX/MO/pdf_files/Astrobotic%20-%20Payload%20User%20Guide%20v3%202018-10.pdf)).
- **Anomaly:** during propulsion commissioning, helium pressure control valve PCV2 failed to reseal. Helium flowed uncontrollably into an oxidizer tank, which over-pressurized, ruptured and leaked oxidizer. This "inhibited the propulsion system from pressurizing to the levels needed for Peregrine to land on the Moon" ([PM1 Post-Mission Report](https://astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PM1_Post-Mission-Report_2024-1.pdf)).
- **Root cause:** the review board was chaired by Dr. John Horack of Ohio State University and had 34 members. It concluded the most likely cause was PCV2 "loss-of-seal capability, most likely due to a mechanical failure caused by vibration-initiated relaxation between threaded components internal to the valve." Astrobotic reproduced this failure in ground tests on a spare valve. Corrective action: future landers will use multiple, dissimilar PCVs ([PM1 Post-Mission Report](https://astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PM1_Post-Mission-Report_2024-1.pdf); [SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/independent-review-confirms-bad-valve-doomed-astrobotics-peregrine-1-mission/)).
- **End of mission:** on 13 Jan 2024, in consultation with NASA, Astrobotic decided to end the mission. Peregrine made a controlled re-entry over the South Pacific on 18 Jan 2024. Astrobotic gives the time as 4:04 p.m. ET, after 10 days 14 hours of operations ([PM1 Post-Mission Report](https://astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PM1_Post-Mission-Report_2024-1.pdf); [Astrobotic Update #20](https://www.astrobotic.com/update-20-for-peregrine-mission-one/)).
- **Conflicting re-entry time:** Wikipedia gives 15:59 EST (20:59 UTC) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_Mission_One)). Astrobotic's report gives 4:04 p.m. ET.
- **What this means for the specs:** neither the slope criterion nor the payload capacity was ever tested at the Moon. The report's subsystem table says terrain-relative navigation was "Not exercised for descent and landing in a lunar environment."
