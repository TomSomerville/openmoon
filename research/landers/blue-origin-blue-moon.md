## Landing slope tolerance

### Mark 1
- **Not published.** I found no slope figure for MK1 from Blue Origin, NASA or a press source.
- Searches run: "Blue Moon lander landing slope degrees tolerance terrain"; "Blue Moon Mark 1 / MK1 lander slope hazard detection Shackleton connecting ridge degrees"; "Human Landing System requirements landing slope degrees NTRS". I also checked the NASA thermal-vacuum article ([NASA, 4 May 2026](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/blue-origin-moon-lander-completes-testing-at-nasa-vacuum-chamber/)), which gives no slope or terrain details, and the Wikipedia pages on [Blue Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(spacecraft)) and [Pathfinder M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_Pathfinder_Mission_1).
- **Historical figure, different vehicle:** at the May 2019 unveiling, the earlier *Blue Moon* concept (rated at 3.6–6.5 t of payload) was described as "capable of landing on inclines up to 15 degrees" ([TechCrunch, 9 May 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/09/blue-origin-unveils-its-lunar-lander-blue-moon/)). That 2019 design predates and differs from today's MK1 (3 t, 8.05 m tall). Nothing I found says the 15° figure still applies to MK1 or MK2, so it should not be quoted as a current MK1 spec.

### Mark 2
- **No terrain-slope design value has been published** for MK2.
- **NASA post-landing tilt limit, 8°:** the OIG says the tilt tolerance for both Starship and Blue Moon, "which NASA established as not to exceed 8 degrees to support all post-landing crew activities", could affect equipment such as the crew hatch if exceeded. The same report notes that south-pole slopes reach "up to 20 degrees" ([NASA OIG IG-26-004, p. 24](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf)). This is a requirement on the lander's attitude after landing. It is not a stated terrain-slope capability.
- **NASA landing-site criterion, < 10°:** NASA's 2024 HLS briefing lists among the "Key Artemis III Landing Requirements" a "Surface slope for landing (100m): <10 deg" and "Surface slope limit for EVA (2km): <20 deg" ([NASA LaRC/ARC EDL seminar, Artemis HLS Update 2024](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240009596/downloads/ArtemisHLSUpdate_2024.pdf)). This is a program-level site-selection constraint, written when Artemis III was a Starship landing. It is not a Blue Moon vehicle spec.
- **Status:** all of these are requirements or design values. No Blue Moon landing has been demonstrated.

## Payload capacity

### Mark 1
- **3,000 kg (3 t), design.** Blue Origin: MK1 "delivers 3 metric tons of payload with high precision anywhere on the lunar surface, day or night, with a single reusable New Glenn launch" ([Blue Origin Blue Moon page](https://www.blueorigin.com/blue-moon), via search snippet; see also [Blue Origin MK1 page](https://www.blueorigin.com/blue-moon/mark-1)).
- The same figure appears in Wikipedia (payload 3,000 kg; fuelled mass 21,350 kg; 8.05 m × 3.08 m) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(spacecraft))) and in Space.com ("up to 3.3 tons (3 metric tons)") ([Space.com, 4 Jun 2025](https://www.space.com/astronomy/moon/lunar-landers-and-transporter-tankers-blue-origin-unveils-its-blueprint-for-the-moon)). The sources agree.
- **Demonstrated:** none. The first flight carries only two small NASA payloads, SCALPSS cameras and a laser retroreflector array ([NASA Science CT-3](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/ct-3-science/); [NASA, 4 May 2026](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/blue-origin-moon-lander-completes-testing-at-nasa-vacuum-chamber/)).

- **Demonstrated:** none.

## Landing record

- **Mark 1:** no launches or landings. The fleet table lists MK1-101 *Endurance* as active with 0 flights ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(spacecraft))). Environmental testing in Chamber A at NASA JSC finished in May 2026, and NASA then said the mission would fly "this year" ([NASA, 4 May 2026](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/blue-origin-moon-lander-completes-testing-at-nasa-vacuum-chamber/)). That has slipped: Pathfinder Mission 1 (CLPS CT-3, to the Shackleton connecting ridge) is now listed as NET Q1 2027 from LC-36 ([Wikipedia: Pathfinder M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_Pathfinder_Mission_1)). It is to show the BE-7, cryogenic systems, avionics, continuous downlink and landing within 100 m of the target. A second MK1 is to deliver NASA's VIPER rover; NASA announced this in Sep 2025 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-blue-origin-to-deliver-viper-rover-to-moons-south-pole/); [Wikipedia: Pathfinder M2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_Pathfinder_Mission_2)). Blue Origin plans two MK1 missions before the MK2 demo ([NASA OIG, p. 8](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf)).
- **New Glenn issues in 2026** ([Wikipedia: New Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Glenn)):
  - On 19 Apr 2026 an upper-stage malfunction left BlueBird 7 in a lower-than-planned orbit. It was the most recent New Glenn launch.
  - On 28 May 2026 a New Glenn exploded during a static fire and severely damaged LC-36A, Blue Origin's only operational launch site. Industry sources cited by Ars Technica said repairs could take more than a year.
  - Wikipedia's launch list says the FAA cleared New Glenn to return to flight on 22 May ([List of New Glenn launches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_Glenn_launches)).
  - NASA's Oct 2026 IAC deck lists an "LC-36B Rebuild" ([NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260009199/downloads/HLS%20IAC%202026%20Presentation%20FINAL.pdf)).
- **Mark 2:** no flights.
  - Schedule: CDR slipped from Dec 2024 to Aug 2025, with a further slip to Jul 2026 expected. The uncrewed demo is expected in Feb 2029 ([NASA OIG, Mar 2026](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/final-report-ig-26-004-nasas-management-of-the-human-landing-system-contracts.pdf)).
  - Hardware: a full-scale crew-cabin prototype was delivered to JSC in 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(spacecraft))).
  - **Conflicting newer plan:** on 4 Nov 2026 NASA said a Blue Moon test article will dock with Orion in Earth orbit on Artemis III (2027). Mark 2 will then be pushed toward the Moon by transfer stages for Artemis IV, and both providers are "targeting to be able to support a crewed lunar landing in 2028" ([NASA, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-shares-plans-to-accelerate-artemis-return-to-moon/)). Wikipedia calls the Artemis III vehicle "Mark 2-D", a crew cabin with ECLSS and RCS but no main propulsion ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(spacecraft))).
