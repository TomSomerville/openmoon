## Landing slope tolerance

**Design spec: not published.** I searched Firefly's Blue Ghost product page, the Mission 1 mission page, the live-updates and landing press releases, the NASA CLPS Mission 1 press kit, Wikipedia (both Blue Ghost articles), NASA NTRS (the SCALPSS 1.1 mission overview) and general web searches. Search terms included "slope tolerance", "maximum slope", "15 degrees" and "landing gear slope requirement". None of these sources gives a maximum slope in degrees for touchdown.

- **Site-selection requirement (the closest published figure).** The 2022 landing-site paper says: "Additional CLPS and lander requirements include slopes ≤10, low risk of obstacles (rocks and craters) >2 m in diameter…". The degree symbol is not printed in the text. The paper also says "Slopes within the ellipse are <~5" and gives the 50 m radius landing ellipse centred at 18.560°N, 61.807°E ([Nagihara et al., LPSC 2022](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2022/pdf/1390.pdf)). This is a requirement for choosing the site, set before flight. It is not a certified tip-over or touchdown limit, and the source does not say how much margin the lander has beyond it.
- **Qualitative design statements (Firefly).** The lander "is designed for stability with shock absorbing feet, a low center of mass, and a wide footprint." Its "four legs utilize crush core honeycomb to absorb shock during touchdown." During descent it used "vision-based terrain relative navigation and hazard avoidance" to find craters, slopes and rocks and pick a hazard-free target ([Firefly mission page](https://fireflyspace.com/missions/blue-ghost-mission-1/); [NASA press kit](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/np-2025-01-001-jsc-clps-firefly-press-kit-screen-508-1-10-25-final.pdf)).
- **Press (not a spec).** Aerospace America says each leg contains a "crush core cartridge" of honeycombed aluminium, intended to help the lander "settle into a reasonably level position," and that "landing at a severe angle would have jeopardized" drilling ([Aerospace America](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/were-on-the-moon-blue-ghost-lander-now-has-14-days-to-learn-all-it-can/)). AmericaSpace says the roughly 3 m leg span "reduces its probability of tipping over if it lands on a slope" but gives no angle ([AmericaSpace](https://www.americaspace.com/2025/01/30/blue-ghost-lander-progresses-through-checkouts/)).
- **Rejected.** A forum estimate of tip-over angle (newmars.com) is amateur work, not a published figure. A Physics World line saying it touched down "at a 30 degree angle" conflicts with every official account of an upright landing and is not used. A 2021 Blue Ghost payload user's guide, marked "Proprietary & Confidential", is circulating on a third-party mirror (satcatalog S3). Firefly does not host it, so per the rules against unofficial mirrors it was not used.

## Payload capacity

- **Design spec (current):** "Mass to surface: Up to 240 kg". With the Elytra transfer vehicle, mass to orbit is up to 2,700 kg ([Firefly Blue Ghost page](https://fireflyspace.com/blue-ghost/), accessed Nov 2026).
- **Conflicting figure:** "150-kilogram payload capacity to the surface" ([TechCrunch, 6 Nov 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/06/fireflys-blue-ghost-lander-represents-a-big-bet-on-a-future-lunar-economy/)). The Wikipedia infobox repeats this figure and cites TechCrunch ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefly_Aerospace_Blue_Ghost)). The two sources do not explain the difference. The Firefly page is newer and official, so it is the preferred source.
- **Demonstrated (Mission 1):** 10 NASA payloads, "collectively expected to total 207 pounds (94 kg)" ([NASA, Feb 2021](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-firefly-aerospace-for-artemis-commercial-moon-delivery-in-2023/)). I did not find a post-flight figure for flown payload mass.
- **Payload power:** > 400 W ([Firefly](https://fireflyspace.com/blue-ghost/)).

## Landing record

| Field | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Date and time | 2 March 2025, 2:34 a.m. CST (about 08:34 UTC) | [Firefly](https://fireflyspace.com/news/firefly-aerospace-becomes-first-commercial-company-to-successfully-land-on-the-moon/); [NASA NTRS SCALPSS paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250010973/downloads/2026_SciTech_SCALPSS_MissionOverview_FINAL.pdf) |
| Site | Mare Crisium, next to Mons Latreille, "within its 100-meter landing target" | [Firefly](https://fireflyspace.com/news/firefly-aerospace-becomes-first-commercial-company-to-successfully-land-on-the-moon/) |
| Resting position (LRO) | 18.5623°N, 61.8103°E, -3650 m elevation, on the rim of a 12 m diameter crater | [LROC, M. Robinson, 7 Mar 2025](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1407) |
| Terrain slope at site | Pre-landing analysis: slopes in the ellipse "<~5" (degrees implied). I found no published post-landing measurement of slope at the touchdown point. | [LPSC 2022](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2022/pdf/1390.pdf) |
| Hazard avoidance | The vision navigation system tracked "craters, slopes, and rocks to select a hazard free parking spot" | [Firefly live updates](https://fireflyspace.com/news/blue-ghost-mission-1-live-updates/) |
| Resting tilt | **Not published as a number.** Firefly says "inertial readings confirmed the lander is upright in a stable configuration." Only three of four footpad contact sensors showed contact on the live display. | [Firefly](https://fireflyspace.com/news/firefly-aerospace-becomes-first-commercial-company-to-successfully-land-on-the-moon/); [Aerospace America](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/were-on-the-moon-blue-ghost-lander-now-has-14-days-to-learn-all-it-can/) |
| Outcome | Success. The lander ran 14+ days of surface operations, returned more than 119 GB of data (51 GB science and technology), and sent its final data around 6:15 pm CDT on 16 March 2025. All 10 NASA payloads collected data. | [Firefly](https://fireflyspace.com/news/firefly-aerospace-successfully-completes-14-days-of-surface-operations-on-the-moon); [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-science-continues-after-fireflys-first-moon-mission-concludes/) |

**What it tests:** The landing shows the lander can touch down upright on terrain that pre-flight analysis put at under about 5°. It does not test the ≤10° site requirement or any higher tolerance, because no resting tilt or touchdown slope has been published.
