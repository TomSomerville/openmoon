## Landing slope tolerance
**Not published.** I did not find a design or certified maximum landing slope for the Series 1 lander in any source.

What I searched:
- **ispace's official pages:** The [July 2020 final-design release](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2020/07/30/commercial-lunar-exploration-program-hakuto-r-reveals-final-design-and-plan-for-mission-1-lunar-lander/) gives mass, size and payload but no slope, tilt or hazard-avoidance figure. The [26 Apr 2023 status update](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/04/26/status-update-on-ispace-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-lander/) and [26 May 2023 results](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/05/26/ispace-announces-results-of-the-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-landing/) do not mention slope either.
- **Wikipedia:** The [Hakuto-R Mission 1 article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuto-R_Mission_1) gives no slope figure.
- **Press:** [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2022/11/29/japanese-commercial-moon-lander-uae-rover-ready-for-launch-on-spacex-rocket/) gives none.
- **Japanese-language search:** I searched for 着陸 傾斜 許容 着陸脚 (landing, slope, tolerance, landing legs). The [MONOist article on the 2020 design](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/articles/2007/31/news037.html) covers the Citizen "Super Titanium" leg parts but gives no slope figure.
- **Payload user's guide:** I found no public Series 1 guide. ispace-U.S.'s [APEX 1.0 Payload User's Guide](https://assets.ispace-us.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/28161539/APEX-PUG-v01.pdf) covers a different, later lander, so it is not used here.

Rejected source: a forum post (orbiter-forum.com) came up in search quoting "slope of the terrain at the crater rim of approximately 15°" with a "two-step landing". I could not open it (HTTP 403), and its wording matches JAXA's SLIM mission, not HAKUTO-R. Generic slope figures (5–15°, 10°) in a [USPTO patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9141113) and an [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.09309) are not about this lander.

## Payload capacity
- **Design target: 30 kg.** The [ispace final-design release (30 Jul 2020)](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2020/07/30/commercial-lunar-exploration-program-hakuto-r-reveals-final-design-and-plan-for-mission-1-lunar-lander/) says "The target payload capacity of 30kg remained unchanged in the final design". Its footnote calls this a total estimated payload capacity as of July 2020, part of which was to be sold to customers.
- **Lander:** The same release gives a dry mass of about 340 kg, a height of 2.3 m and a width of 2.6 m with legs extended.
- **Launch mass:** About 1 t fully fuelled ([Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2022/11/29/japanese-commercial-moon-lander-uae-rover-ready-for-launch-on-spacex-rocket/)). Wikipedia gives about 1,000 kg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuto-R_Mission_1)).
- **Flown on Mission 1:** About 11 kg (24 lb) of customer payloads. "the rover takes up most of the Hakuto-R lander's payload capacity" ([Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2022/11/29/japanese-commercial-moon-lander-uae-rover-ready-for-launch-on-spacex-rocket/)). The rover is the UAE's Rashid. The other payloads were JAXA/Tomy's SORA-Q and others ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuto-R_Mission_1)).
- **Delivered to the surface:** Nothing. The landing failed.
- **Conflicts:** None found. The 30 kg figure is a pre-flight design target and was never shown in flight.

## Landing record
| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | 11 Dec 2022, Falcon 9, CCSFS SLC-40 | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuto-R_Mission_1) |
| Descent start | 26 Apr 2023 00:40 JST (25 Apr 15:40 UTC). Touchdown was scheduled for about 01:40–01:43 JST. | [ispace results](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/05/26/ispace-announces-results-of-the-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-landing/); [ispace status update](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/04/26/status-update-on-ispace-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-lander/) |
| Target site | Atlas crater, Mare Frigoris. Wikipedia gives the site as 47°34′52″N 44°05′38″E. ispace says the landing site was modified after the critical design review in February 2021. | [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2022/11/29/japanese-commercial-moon-lander-uae-rover-ready-for-launch-on-spacex-rocket/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuto-R_Mission_1); [ispace results](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/05/26/ispace-announces-results-of-the-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-landing/) |
| Terrain slope at site | Not published. ispace describes only a "large cliff approximately 3 kms in elevation", the crater rim, on the approach path. No slope value was given for the touchdown point. | [ispace results](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/05/26/ispace-announces-results-of-the-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-landing/) |
| Outcome | Failed and crashed. The lander kept a vertical attitude during the final approach. After its propellant ran low, its descent speed rose and contact was lost. | [ispace status update](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/04/26/status-update-on-ispace-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-lander/) |
| Cause | Software, not hardware. The altitude sensor reading rose sharply as the lander passed over the crater rim. The onboard software wrongly judged this an abnormal sensor value and rejected the sensor's data. The lander then estimated its altitude as zero while it was actually about 5 km up. It used up its propellant, and controlled descent ceased; ispace believes it free-fell to the surface. Pre-flight simulations "did not adequately incorporate the lunar environment on the navigation route." | [ispace results](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/05/26/ispace-announces-results-of-the-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-landing/) |
| Impact site | LRO NAC imaged it on 26 Apr 2023 at 47.581°N, 44.094°E, showing at least four prominent pieces of debris. | [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/lro/nasas-lro-views-impact-site-of-hakuto-r-mission-1-moon-lander/) |

**What this says about the slope claim:** It cannot be tested. The lander never reached a controlled touchdown, so its leg and slope tolerance was never exercised. The failure happened in navigation and guidance software over a crater rim, not on landing-site slope.

Context, not part of this lander's record: ispace's Mission 2 "RESILIENCE" lander was also lost during its landing attempt in Mare Frigoris on 5 June 2025 ([NASASpaceFlight](https://nasaspaceflight.com/2025/06/hakuto-r-m2-landing/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuto-R_Mission_2)).
