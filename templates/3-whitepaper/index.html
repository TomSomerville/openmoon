Chapter 1.2 · Phase 1, the robotic outpost

Power through the lunar night

Sample content: this chapter shows the layout. The figures are placeholders and have not been checked against sources yet.

Every system in the outpost depends on electricity, and the Moon makes electricity hard. At most latitudes the Sun sets for about fourteen Earth days at a time, so solar arrays alone would need two weeks of stored energy. This chapter describes the outpost's answer: a small fission reactor for base load, supported by solar arrays and batteries.

Why not solar only? A few crater rims near the south pole see sunlight most of the time. The plan uses them, but they are small and few.

1.2.1The parts of the reactor

The power unit breaks down into five sub-components. Each has its own section later in this chapter, with its parts and their problems.

  1. Reactor coreFuel, neutron reflector and control drums.
  2. Power conversionStirling or Brayton engines that turn heat into electricity.
  3. Heat rejectionRadiators that send waste heat out to space.
  4. Radiation shieldingA shadow shield, a regolith berm and a keep-out distance.
  5. Power transmissionA high-voltage cable to the outpost and the electronics at each end.
The reactor is the one part of the outpost that has to work on the first night.

1.2.2What could go wrong, and the fix

CriticalThe night is about fourteen days long

Batteries sized for two weeks would be very heavy. Solution: the reactor carries base load, and batteries cover only short peaks.

HighDust settles on the radiators

Solution: stand the radiators vertically and place the unit well away from landing pads.

Open question. How far must landing pads be from the reactor? Contributions welcome.

HighRadiation at the outpost

Solution: a regolith berm and a keep-out distance, so the terrain adds shielding.
